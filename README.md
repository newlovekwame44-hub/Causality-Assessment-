# Causality Assessment of an Adverse Drug Reaction (Task 3)

## The case

A 45-year-old woman got a skin rash three days after she started amoxicillin. The rash went away once the drug was stopped. I used the WHO-UMC categories (Certain, Probable, Possible) to work out how likely it is that amoxicillin caused it.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Patient | 45-year-old female |
| Suspected drug | Amoxicillin |
| Adverse reaction | Skin rash |
| Onset | 3 days after starting the drug |
| Outcome | Recovered after stopping the drug |

## Going through the criteria

**Temporal relationship.** The rash came after the first dose, so the order makes sense. Three days is a normal window for a skin reaction to a penicillin, so this criterion is met.

**Dechallenge.** She got better when the drug was withdrawn. This is the strongest evidence in the case.

**Alternative causes.** The case doesn't mention any other drug, infection or condition that could explain the rash, so I assumed there was none. This is the weakest part of the assessment. Viral infections often cause rashes on their own, and a real case would need that ruled out first.

**Rechallenge.** It wasn't done, and it wouldn't be appropriate anyway. Giving someone a suspected allergen again just to confirm a diagnosis is rarely ethical.

## Classification

**Probable**

| Category | What it needs | Fits? |
|---|---|---|
| Certain | Reasonable timing, positive dechallenge, and a positive rechallenge or a clearly drug-specific event | No, no rechallenge |
| Probable | Reasonable timing, a sensible response to withdrawal, and other causes unlikely | Yes |
| Possible | Reasonable timing, but disease or other drugs could also explain it | Too weak for this case |

The timing fits, the rash cleared when amoxicillin was stopped, and nothing points to another cause, which is what Probable asks for. It can't be Certain without a rechallenge. It's more than Possible because the dechallenge was clearly positive.

## What this assessment can't tell us

If a viral infection or another new medicine showed up in the history, I would lower the rating to Possible. The case also gives no description of the rash (type, where it was, how severe), so I couldn't check whether it looks like a typical amoxicillin rash.

## Follow-up

The reaction should go in the patient's record as a suspected amoxicillin allergy. She should avoid amoxicillin and be careful with other penicillins because of cross-reactivity. The case should also be reported through the local pharmacovigilance system.
